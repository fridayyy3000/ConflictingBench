# GOV-RAG: Borderline Today, Submittable in Three Weeks

GOV-RAG can clear every hard gate of the NAACL 2027 System Demonstrations call, but only if the team stops treating the 30-question ConflictBench Fictional pack as its evidence and spends the 20 days before **4 December 2026 (11:59 pm UTC-12)** on four things in this order: a live, reachable URL (desk-reject without it), a fix to the two pipeline bugs that currently generate the headline numbers (the citation validator and the authority weight that fills six of eight context slots with irrelevant documents), an external evaluation on **RAMDocs (500 q), CONFLICTS (458 q), WikiContradict (253 q) and FaithEval-Inconsistent (1.5K)** against vanilla RAG, majority vote and a re-implemented Astute RAG, and a six-page paper that foregrounds the governance-status inference, the four-way decision head and the inspectable-evidence UI rather than the LLM plumbing. The confirmed CFP requires a 2.5-minute screencast, a live demo link, "any form of evaluation", a license statement and a generative-AI disclosure, and explicitly says systems that "mainly wrap existing APIs or assemble off-the-shelf models without substantive contribution will not be accepted" ([NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)). The current 29/30 vs 17/30 result carries a roughly **±18-point 95% confidence interval**, both GOV-RAG misses and 10 of 13 baseline misses are validator rejections rather than wrong answers, the authority phrases in the documents are the same templates the regex scorer detects, and every question has exactly one gold answer so the three abstention decisions are never scorable as correct; none of that survives a reviewer who opens the demo. No public dataset encodes document status (active / draft / superseded) as a field, and no published method infers governance status as a credibility signal, so the status-aware reranker is a defensible novelty claim; the literature's closest analogue, Credibility-aware Generation, treats source authority as a fixed per-site constant and shows that replacing heuristic labels with gold ones lifts EM from 0.466 to 0.610 ([CAG, arXiv 2404.06809](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.06809v3)). Demo tracks accept 34–38% of submissions and lose 5–8% of entrants before review for missing materials ([2026.acl-demo.0 front matter](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-demo.0.pdf)), so operational discipline, not a new algorithm, is what converts a borderline draft into an accepted paper.

## Four external datasets replace the 30-question pilot; one unverified corpus could become a contribution

The research notes catalogue roughly 30 public resources from 2021 to September 2026, and the ranking below is driven by three questions that match GOV-RAG's design: does the dataset expose per-document labels so that *document selection* (not just answer accuracy) can be scored, does it ever score `unresolved_conflict` or another abstention as correct, and does it test "the authoritative or newer document wins", which is the behaviour the governance reranker exists to produce. Only a handful of sets reward reporting a conflict (WikiContradict, FaithEval-Inconsistent, CONFLICTS, Ragability, LIT-RAGBench), only a handful carry per-document gold/misinformation/noise labels (RAMDocs, MacNoise, RAGuard, CONFACT, HoH, QACC, ConflictBank, ClashEval, ConflictingQA), and only the 2025–26 temporal sets test newer-wins ([Knowledge Conflicts survey, EMNLP 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.08319v2); [Ragability, LREC 2026](http://www.lrec-conf.org/proceedings/lrec2026/pdf/2026.lrec2026-1.182.pdf)). The decisive gap for GOV-RAG is that **no dataset anywhere in the 2023–2026 literature exposes a status field such as active / draft / superseded**, and no benchmark labels per instance whether a conflict is resolvable by authority or genuinely unresolved, except CONFLICTS' query-level taxonomy ([CONFLICTS, arXiv 2506.08500v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08500v2)).

Below the table sit sets that should be cited but not run. AmbigDocs (36,098 q) and WhoQA (5,152 q) score a clarification request as a failure, so they punish `needs_information`; gpt-3.5-turbo asked for clarification on 99% of hard WhoQA questions and was scored as rejecting them ([WhoQA, arXiv 2410.15737](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.15737); [AmbigDocs](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.12447v3)). ConflictBank's 7.45 million claim–evidence pairs are synthetic multiple-choice and scored by memorisation ratio rather than by rewarding its "uncertain" option ([arXiv 2408.12076](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.12076)). ClashEval, ConflictQA (Xie et al.) and the KNOWLEDGE CONFLICT framework measure context-versus-memory conflict, not document-versus-document conflict ([ClashEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10198); [ConflictQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13300)). MacNoise, RAGuard and CONFACT give per-document perturbed or misleading labels but their gold is always the true answer, so they measure robustness rather than calibrated abstention; CONFACT is nonetheless the only set with a per-document 1–5 credibility label and shows credibility signals add 1–4 points ([CONFACT, arXiv 2505.17762](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17762)). Stale-Document Poisoning (317 dated official reversals in medicine, law, software and platform policy) is the closest domain match but has no data link on its arXiv page ([arXiv 2609.31342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31342)); VersionQA's 100 questions over 34 versioned technical documents have no confirmed public release ([arXiv 2510.08109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08109)); "Asking For An Old Friend" (312 German statutory QA pairs, ICAIL 2026) documents the recency-bias failure GOV-RAG should avoid, but its repository URL comes from a search snippet ([arXiv 2605.23497](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23497)). The notes mark "ConfliBERT" and "MultiConflict" as names for which no conflicting-evidence dataset exists, and the blog-cited "ERAA-2026" and "TemporalRAG-Bench 2026" as possibly non-existent; none should appear in the paper.

The one dataset the team could *create* in this window is also the one that would most strengthen the novelty claim. **IETF RFCs and Python PEPs encode supersession as explicit, machine-readable graph edges with permissive terms**: the single `rfc-index.xml` file holds 9,858 RFC entries of which 1,384 carry `obsoleted-by` and 1,246 carry `updated-by`, with `current-status` values (PROPOSED STANDARD 4,438; INFORMATIONAL 3,013; HISTORIC 353; INTERNET STANDARD 133) that map onto active / superseded / archived, while `peps.json` holds 749 PEPs with status counts Final 375, Draft 57, Active 39, Superseded 25 and 31 `superseded_by` links, each PEP placed in the public domain or under CC0 ([RFC index](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc-index.xml); [PEP 1](https://peps.python.org/pep-0001/); counts computed locally in the notes on 2026-11-14). The RFC "updates" relation, a partial amendment where both documents stay active, is a labelled test of scope-of-applicability, and Internet-Drafts are genuine draft-versus-published distractors; no published paper has used either corpus for QA under conflict ([RFC 7322](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7322.html)). The catch is the IETF Trust Legal Provisions: the notes could not read TLP 5.0's reproduction clause, so verify it before redistributing RFC text; PEPs carry no such risk. A 200–300 question set here would answer the circularity critique with real documents whose authority phrases were written by standards editors, not by the team's templates.

## Vanilla RAG, majority vote and Astute RAG are the baselines reviewers will expect; MADAM-RAG is the published bar

Across the method literature the de-facto baseline set is No-RAG closed book, vanilla concatenated RAG, majority vote or self-consistency over passages, and then one or two published conflict methods ([Astute RAG, arXiv 2410.07176v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07176v1); [MADAM-RAG, arXiv 2504.13079v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13079v2)). GOV-RAG built its first plain-RAG baseline yesterday; it still lacks a closed-book row, a majority-vote row and any published method. Majority vote matters specifically because it is the failure GOV-RAG's one-chunk-per-claim diversification is designed to defeat: it is competitive on real conflicts (QACC Phi-3: 52.52 EM vs 49.57 plain context) but collapses under source imbalance (RA-RAG NQ accuracy 0.740 falls to 0.327 at seven adversarial sources of nine) ([QACC](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12311v3); [RA-RAG, arXiv 2410.22954v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.22954v5)). Showing GOV-RAG beating majority vote where majority vote is wrong is the single most convincing figure the paper can contain.

Three facts about the published bar shape what the paper can claim. First, **gains on realistic inter-document conflict are small**: MADAM-RAG improves on concatenated prompting by only +1.8 EM on RAMDocs for Llama-3.3-70B, against +15.8 on FaithEval, and the clearest pattern in the literature is a +30–40 EM gain on synthetic context-memory conflict versus +2–6 on real document conflict ([MADAM-RAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13079v2)). A 12-point swing on 30 synthetic questions will therefore read as a synthetic-benchmark artefact unless it is reproduced, at smaller magnitude, on RAMDocs or CONFLICTS. Second, the strongest published method is fragile: an independent 2026 re-implementation found MADAM-RAG reaching only 56.6% clean accuracy versus 92.0% for vanilla RAG with a 41.4% non-answer rate, and consuming 72% of the study's compute ([Korn, arXiv 2605.05632](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05632)). That is an argument for running Astute RAG as the primary method baseline and MADAM-RAG only if budget remains, and for scoring GOV-RAG's own abstention decisions with a coverage penalty so that over-abstention cannot masquerade as caution. Third, discern-style prompting *hurt* GPT-4o and Claude-3 on naturally conflicting web contexts (48.71 to 40.71) ([QACC](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12311v3)), so a prompt-only "detect then answer" variant is a legitimate and cheap baseline that GOV-RAG should beat.

On novelty, the literature splits credibility signals into hand rules on real metadata (CAG: publication date against an LLM-voted validity period, per-scenario source reliability), LLM-extracted criteria (ConflictRAG: authority 0.312 and recency 0.245 entropy weights), and label-free agreement estimation (RA-RAG) ([CAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.06809v3); [ConflictRAG, arXiv 2605.17301v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17301v1); [RA-RAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.22954v5)). Nothing in that set uses document status words such as "DRAFT", "supersedes" or "for information only", and RA-RAG's agreement heuristic is structurally wrong for supersession because the superseded and current documents disagree by design and derivative summaries of the old policy would outvote the single current one; Jin et al.'s "majority rule" finding, that models trust evidence appearing more frequently, is the mechanism ([Jin et al., arXiv 2402.14409](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14409)). The closest accepted demos to position against are VeraCT Scan (ACL 2024, corroborating versus conflicting reports weighted by source credibility), FACTS&EVIDENCE (NAACL 2025, per-claim credibility with diverse evidence), PRAISE (ACL 2025, contradictions between two sources with evidence), TruthReader (EMNLP 2024, inline citations plus refusal recall) and KMatrix-2 (EMNLP 2025, toolkit-level inter-context conflict resolution); none is about conflict-aware RAG for policy documents, so the niche is open ([2024.acl-demos.25](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-demos.25.pdf); [2025.naacl-demo.35](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-demo.35.pdf); [2025.acl-demo.62](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-demo.62.pdf); [2024.emnlp-demo.10](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-demo.10.pdf); [2025.emnlp-demos.42](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-demos.42.pdf)).

## Today's pipeline clears the desk-reject gates only once a URL is live, and would then be judged thin

The CFP is confirmed and leaves little room for interpretation. Submission is via OpenReview group `aclweb.org/NAACL/2027/Demo_Track`, single-blind, **6 content pages plus a 2-page appendix cap**, unlimited references and ethics, a **required screencast of up to 2.5 minutes**, a **required live-demo or download link ("submissions without one are desk rejected")**, a **required evaluation ("submissions that fail to provide any form of evaluation will be desk rejected")**, a required license statement, a generative-AI disclosure in the acknowledgements, no rebuttal, notification 10 February 2027, camera-ready 3 March 2027, and the conference in San Francisco on 1–5 June 2027 ([NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)). Relative to NAACL 2025 the link moved from "strongly recommended" to desk-reject, the evaluation from unstated to desk-reject, and the appendix from unlimited to two pages ([NAACL 2025 demo CFP](https://2025.naacl.org/calls/demo/)). Reviewers "are expected to interact with the demo", so the URL must stay up from December through February, and the ACL 2025 chairs complained about text-to-speech and silent videos and recommended banning them, so the narration should be human ([ACL 2025Q3 Demo Chairs report](https://www.aclweb.org/adminwiki/index.php/2025Q3_Reports:_Demo_Chairs)). Acceptance rates have held at 34–38% at ACL and EMNLP 2024–2026 (ACL 2026: 85 of 227) and 43% at the small NAACL 2024 track ([2026.acl-demo.0](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-demo.0.pdf); [2024.naacl-demo.0](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-demo.0.pdf)).

Against that rubric the frank verdict is that **GOV-RAG today is a strong prototype with weak evidence and no reachable deployment**. The gates it passes: it is a working FastAPI system with a web UI, it has a measurable contribution that is not plumbing (governance metadata, authority-weighted reranking, claim-level diversification, a four-way decision head, a verbatim-citation validator), and it has a comparison to a baseline. The gates it does not yet pass or passes weakly are four. There is no live URL, and the README describes an earlier dataset while code is duplicated across three folders, which is exactly the "incomplete system" impression a reviewer forms when the hosted demo and the paper disagree. The evaluation rests on 30 LLM-generated questions where, by Miller's binary standard error, **n = 30 at p around 0.5 gives a 95% interval of about ±18 points**, n = 100 about ±10, n = 500 about ±4.4 and n = 1,000 about ±3.1, and roughly 969 questions are needed to detect a 3-point difference at 80% power ([Miller, arXiv 2411.00640](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00640v1)); published conflict benchmarks sit in the 250–500 range as a de-facto floor. The benchmark is circular: the authority phrases in the documents are the templates the regex scorer looks for, and the authority-bias literature shows models already follow such surface cues, so the test measures template recognition rather than credibility reasoning ([ACL 2025 long 1400](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2025.acl-long.1400); [Trust Me, I'm an Expert, arXiv 2601.13433](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2601.13433)). And the decision head is unevaluated: every question has one gold and "answered" is the only correct decision, so `unresolved_conflict`, `needs_information` and `insufficient_evidence` have never been scored, which means the paper cannot currently say anything about the feature that most distinguishes it from a plain RAG chatbot.

The API-wrapper risk is real but manageable. The LLM (Nemotron 3.5 Lightning 30B) and embeddings (bge-small) are off the shelf, and a reviewer who sees "semantic retrieval, rerank, prompt an LLM" will reach for the rejection clause. What rebuts it is the part no published system has: status inference from document text, the authority-versus-relevance trade-off, the diversification that defeats majority crowding, the validator, and a UI that lets the user inspect governing versus superseded evidence. The paper must spend its Figure 1 and its first two pages on those, and the ablation table must show that removing each one costs accuracy. The known weakness that authority outranks relevance, so that authoritative documents from unrelated questions fill six of eight context slots, is the other reviewer-visible hazard: a reviewer who types a question the corpus does not cover will see irrelevant "authoritative" chunks surfaced with confidence. Fixing this is a correctness issue for the live demo, not only an evaluation issue.

## Fix the validator and the authority weight first, then make abstention scorable

The two headline misses and ten of thirteen baseline misses are citation-validator rejections, most likely from dropped Markdown bold markers, so the reported 29/30 versus 17/30 is partly a bug differential rather than a method differential. The fix is deterministic quote verification on *normalised* text (strip Markdown, collapse whitespace, normalise quotes and dashes, then substring match), reported as a quote-verification rate, which is how practitioners do it and how the one adjacent benchmark (CoVeGAT, 500 genuine and 500 minimally perturbed claim–quote pairs, 96.4% for a MiniLM baseline) frames it ([CoVeGAT](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2025.r2lm-1.1); [Vaquill engineering note](https://www.vaquill.ai/engineering/verify-ai-citations-in-code)). For statements that are not verbatim, layer ALCE citation recall and precision with an NLI model, and human-audit at least 100 statements reporting agreement, mirroring ALCE's 85.1% recall and 77.6% precision agreement and CONFLICTS' 0.89 on 100 items ([ALCE, arXiv 2305.14627](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.14627); [CONFLICTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08500v2)). Rerun both systems after the fix before any other number is quoted.

The reranker's `0.30 semantic + 0.50 authority + 0.20 scope` is the second bug. Authority should never promote a chunk that is not relevant; the published pattern that works is CAG's `credibility = min(adjusted relevance, source)`, where source authority can only cap, never lift, a relevance tier ([CAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.06809v3)). Concretely, gate on a relevance threshold or on rank within the semantic top-k, then apply authority as the tiebreaker inside that set, and report the fraction of final-context chunks that are topically relevant as a diagnostic. Add a **recency or effective-date signal**, because today the pipeline cannot run HoH or the CONFLICTS Outdated subset meaningfully, and because the only method found that uses publication date against an estimated validity period is CAG. But build the evaluation so recency does not become the whole story: statutory QA shows a two-sided failure, applying superseded rules after amendment *and* preferring newer provisions when a historical version governs ([Asking For An Old Friend](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23497)), and TIDE found models detect that a supplied version does not govern only 26.7% of the time against 59.7% for resolving the right version from an implicit date ([TIDE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08512)). Status should beat raw recency on draft-versus-final and summary-versus-source cases, and the recency-only baseline is how the paper proves it.

Claim extraction is numeric-only, so diversification is off for the 12 of 30 date, entity, categorical and boolean questions. Extend the extractor to dates and entities (spaCy-style PER/ORG/LOC/DATE/NUM, the same span types Longpre-style perturbation uses), or replace the regex with one LLM call that lists the candidate answers across chunks; either way the diversification should run on every question type and the ablation should report "diversification off" as a row. The authority rules themselves are keyword templates and should stay so for the demo (there is no training data), but two changes make them defensible: an **ablation that strips or shuffles authority phrases** in the documents and shows the decision degrades gracefully rather than collapsing, which is the remedy implied by the DRUID and Xie critiques of synthetic conflicts ([DRUID, arXiv 2412.17031](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17031); [Xie et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.13300)), and an **oracle-labels row** that bounds how much the heuristics cost, as CAG did.

Finally, make the three abstention decisions scorable. The cheapest route is to extend ConflictBench Fictional with three mirror conditions per question: remove the gold document so the correct decision is `insufficient_evidence`; replace the gold with two documents of equal authority and different answers so the correct decision is `unresolved_conflict`; and rewrite the question to be ambiguous between two scopes so the correct decision is `needs_information`. That turns 30 items into 120 with a balanced four-class decision label, matching the balanced-case design ClashEval argues for because a benchmark that scores only one behaviour can be gamed by always producing it ([ClashEval, NeurIPS 2024](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/3aa291abc426d7a29fb08418c1244177-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf)). Label each item also with whether the gold is the newest document, so a recency-only heuristic is caught. While doing this, generate the questions with fictional or post-cutoff entities (already the case) and keep a closed-book row per item, which is the leakage control RGB and Longpre use ([RGB, arXiv 2309.01431v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.01431v2); [Longpre et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2109.05052v2)).

## An evaluation protocol reviewers will believe: a decision matrix, a decomposition and intervals

The field has no single composite metric for a four-way decision head; the closest analogues are RefusalBench's refusal-category codes (REFUSE_CONTRADICTORY, REFUSE_AMBIGUOUS, REFUSE_MISSING), where a refusal counts as correct only if its category matches and the best frontier model reaches 73.0% refusal accuracy on NQ but 36.1% on GaRAGe, and CONFLICTS' five-way conflict-type accuracy where the best model scores 65.3% ([RefusalBench, arXiv 2510.10390](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10390v1); [CONFLICTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08500v2)). The reviewer-legible design is two layers. The first is a **4×4 decision confusion matrix** with per-class precision, recall and macro-F1, plus the Know Your Limits aggregates with "abstain" defined as any of the three non-answer decisions: abstention precision N5/(N3+N5), abstention recall N5/(N2+N4+N5), coverage, reliable accuracy N1/(N1+N2+N4) and effective reliability (N1−N2−N4)/total ([Know Your Limits, arXiv 2407.18418v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18418v3)). RefusalBench found models default to "missing information" for about a quarter of predictions, so the `insufficient_evidence` row of the matrix should be inspected for over-prediction, not reported as a bare rate. The second layer is content scoring conditioned on the decision: normalised containment with alias lists for atomic answers, RAMDocs' strict set-EM (all gold, no misinformation-only answer) on RAMDocs, WikiContradict's "both answers, no preference, flags the conflict" rubric for `unresolved_conflict`, and CONFLICTS' per-type expected-behaviour adherence. Explicitly state the mapping `unresolved_conflict` to REFUSE_CONTRADICTORY / WikiContradict T5 / CONFLICTS Conflicting-opinions, `insufficient_evidence` to REFUSE_MISSING / FaithEval-Unanswerable, `needs_information` to REFUSE_AMBIGUOUS, so results are comparable, and say plainly that no benchmark scores a clarification request as correct.

Decomposition into retrieval versus resolution failure follows a convention rather than a named metric: report gold-chunk recall@8 (already 30/30 for GOV-RAG and 21/30 for the baseline), then decision accuracy conditioned on gold-in-context and on gold-not-retrieved, following RAGChecker's retriever-versus-generator split and the gold-only / gold-plus-distractors / no-gold conditions ([RAGChecker, arXiv 2408.08067](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.08067); [RAGBench, arXiv 2407.11005v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.11005v2)). For the majority-of-wrong-answers phenomenon there is **no standard name**: define a "majority-trap rate" as P(final answer = the majority-supported wrong answer | a majority of retrieved chunks support a wrong answer and at least one supports the correct one), state that it is analogous to ClashEval's context bias and Xie et al.'s memorisation ratio under controlled evidence counts (ChatGPT's MR rose from 3.7% to 99.8% as support went from 0 of 2 to 2 of 2), and plot it as a dose–response curve over the fraction of wrong-supporting chunks rather than as a single rate, which is what RAMDocs did with 1–3 misinformation documents ([ClashEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.10198v2); [Xie et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.13300); [RAMDocs](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13079v2)). ConflictBench's four-share-one-wrong-answer design is already a fixed point on that curve; add 0, 2 and 6 to make it a curve.

On statistics, report standard errors and 95% intervals for every headline, use paired comparisons on the same questions, and cluster standard errors by source document because questions generated from one document are correlated and naive standard errors can be understated by up to roughly 3× ([Miller](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00640v1)). For the 30-question pack, resample each question K = 5–10 times at the deployment temperature; with n = 198, raising K from 1 to 10 shrank the minimum detectable effect from 13.2 to 7.5 points, and the same logic roughly halves the detectable effect here, though nothing makes 30 items reviewer-credible for headline claims. Use the paired bootstrap or permutation test for significance, as Dror et al. and Berg-Kirkpatrick et al. recommend for paired non-normal metrics ([Dror et al., ACL 2018](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1128/); [Berg-Kirkpatrick et al. 2012](https://nlp.cs.berkeley.edu/pubs/BergKirkpatrick-Burkett-Klein_2012_Significance_paper.pdf)). Where an LLM judge is used (CONFLICTS adherence, WikiContradict rubric), validate it on 100 human-labelled items and report agreement, randomise candidate order and hide system identity, and prefer a judge model different from the Nemotron generator; WikiContradict's own human agreement fell to κ 0.58–0.67 on the conflict templates versus 0.85–0.88 on single-answer templates, so expect the conflict rubric to be the noisiest number in the paper ([WikiContradict](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13805v1)). Lexical EM underestimates human-judged accuracy by about 20 points on NQ-open, half of the misses being semantic equivalents and a fifth list-style answers, which is exactly the shape of multi-answer conflict outputs, so use GPT-4-class or NLI judging for correctness and keep exact match only for the deterministic quote check ([Kamalloo et al., ACL 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.06984)).

A small user study is worth its cost because accepted RAG demos almost always pair a component table with a user, expert or deployment signal, and because the CFP's own list of what strong submissions do names user studies first. The recognisable design is within-subjects, N of 8–12 at minimum (PaperTrail analysed 26; ELLA, My Climate CoPilot and SpiritRAG used small expert groups), two conditions (baseline answer with citations versus decision label with inspectable governing / superseded evidence), tasks with known ground truth so accuracy is measured, and measures of accuracy, time, TXAI trust, behavioural reliance and verification actions, analysed with paired t or Wilcoxon and effect sizes ([PaperTrail, CHI '26](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21045v1); [ELLA](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-demos.36.pdf)). Report behaviour alongside self-reported trust: PaperTrail found claim-level provenance *lowered* trust (4.22 to 3.68, d = 0.44) without changing reliance, and biased result sets moved user accuracy from 43% to 23% or 65%, so the honest expectation is that showing conflict changes calibration more than it changes raw trust ([Pogacar et al. 2017](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~aghenai/assets/publications/pogacar-ictir2017.pdf)).

## A 20-day schedule with explicit cut lines and claims the paper can defend

The schedule below assumes the team's stated compute: an NVIDIA API key with Nemotron 3.5 Lightning 30B at 2–40 s per call, local sentence-transformers, and no GCP credits. GOV-RAG at 27–39 s per question means 500 questions is roughly four to five hours per system per dataset, so with modest concurrency the full external sweep (GOV-RAG plus three cheap baselines on RAMDocs, CONFLICTS, WikiContradict and a 300-item FaithEval sample, about 1,500 items × 4 systems) fits in a few days of wall-clock time; MADAM-RAG does not fit unless everything else is done. Evaluation occupies only 20–25% of an accepted demo paper's content, so the plan deliberately caps evaluation effort and protects the live system and the video ([observed across 21 accepted RAG demos](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-demo.49.pdf)).

The cut order if time runs short is the reverse of value per hour. Drop MADAM-RAG first; it is the most expensive baseline, its 2026 re-implementation already shows it is fragile, and Astute RAG is the baseline the MADAM-RAG authors themselves re-implemented. Drop the RFC/PEP benchmark second, and say in Limitations that a real-corpus supersession benchmark is future work; the IETF licence check alone could consume a day. Drop HoH and QACC third; CONFLICTS' Outdated subset already tests recency on real web pages. Shrink the user study to a five-person expert walkthrough with qualitative feedback fourth, since My Climate CoPilot and SpiritRAG were accepted with expert-only evaluations ([2025.acl-demo.7](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-demo.7.pdf); [2025.emnlp-demos.3](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-demos.3.pdf)). Never cut the live URL, the video, the validator fix, the relevance gate, the 120-item extended ConflictBench with the ablations, or RAMDocs plus CONFLICTS against vanilla and majority vote with confidence intervals; those are the minimum that turns the submission from a wrapper with a pilot into a system with evidence.

The paper should claim five things and avoid five others. Claim that GOV-RAG infers governance status and scope from document text and uses them, gated by relevance, to choose governing evidence, and that no published method uses status language as a credibility signal (CAG's per-site constants are the nearest). Claim that claim-level diversification lowers the majority-trap rate relative to vanilla RAG and majority vote, with a dose–response curve and intervals. Claim that every citation is verbatim-verified and report the verification rate and NLI-based citation recall and precision. Claim a four-way decision with a confusion matrix on balanced data, and report abstention precision and coverage so over-abstention is visible. Claim an inspectable UI, evaluated by a small user study or expert walkthrough. Do not claim state of the art on RAMDocs or any external set; the honest statement is "competitive with or above vanilla RAG and majority vote, below or near MADAM-RAG's 34.40 EM", because the one-gold design will lose RAMDocs items with multiple valid answers. Do not present 29/30 as the headline; present it as a pilot with its interval. Do not call "majority-trap rate" a standard metric. Do not claim generalisation to real policy corpora without the strip-cues ablation and at least one real-document set. Do not claim calibrated abstention until the three non-answer decisions have been scored on data where they are correct. Finally, observe the overlap rule: if a companion ARR paper on the method is planned, keep the demo's content and results more than 75% distinct, since identical work submitted to ARR and the demo track is rejected by both ([NAACL 2025 demo CFP](https://2025.naacl.org/calls/demo/); [NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)).

Items the notes flag as unverified and that should not be stated as fact in the paper: Astute RAG's lack of an official repository (searched, none found); the public release of QACC with its NAACL version and of VersionQA at all; the Ragability dataset licence; the IETF Trust TLP 5.0 reproduction clause; the FaithEval HF schema's separability of the fabricated passage; ConflictRAG's three evaluation benchmarks and TCR's internally inconsistent tables; the existence of "ConfliBERT", "MultiConflict", "ERAA-2026" and "TemporalRAG-Bench 2026" as conflict datasets; the widely repeated "random citations increase trust" AAAI 2025 study; and the per-model numbers of TempoMed-Bench, FRESCO's obsolete ratio and AuthorityBench, all from snippets. The NAACL 2027 CFP details, by contrast, were read from the official page on 14 November 2026 and can be relied on.

## Conclusion

The research reframes GOV-RAG's problem: the pipeline's distinguishing idea, that a document's own governance language should decide which evidence governs, sits in a genuine gap in the literature, but its evidence so far demonstrates template recognition on a benchmark the team wrote to match its own regexes. The three weeks are therefore not about inventing anything; they are about moving the proof onto documents the team did not author, scoring the three abstention decisions somewhere they can be right, and replacing a bug-inflated 12-point swing with a smaller, intervalled, reproduced gain over the two baselines (vanilla concatenation and majority vote) whose failure the method was built to prevent.

Two implications reach beyond the deadline. First, the two-sided temporal failure documented in statutory QA and TIDE, where models both apply superseded rules and over-prefer newer ones, means a status-aware system has a research story that a recency-aware one does not, provided the evaluation includes cases where the older or less recent document governs; the RFC and PEP corpora, with their explicit obsoletes, updates and superseded_by edges and permissive terms, are the natural home for that benchmark after NAACL. Second, CAG's gold-versus-heuristic gap of 14 EM points says the ceiling on this approach is set by label precision, not by the resolver model, so the long-run investment is a learned or human-validated governance labeller rather than a larger LLM, and the demo paper should say so in its Limitations as the roadmap rather than as an apology.
