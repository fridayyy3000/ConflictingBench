## KQ1. Published methods that estimate source credibility / reliability for RAG; how labels are produced and how much they help

### Takeaway
Credibility signals in the published RAG literature are almost always *constructed automatically* (retriever relevance tiers, query-time-minus-publication-date gaps, site reputation lists, or cross-source agreement) rather than human-labelled; the two best-documented systems (CAG, RA-RAG) report large gains over vanilla RAG but CAG explicitly shows that label precision is the bottleneck (gold labels add +14 EM points over heuristic labels). Separately, controlled studies show LLMs weight *relevance* far more than *style* or *credibility cues*, but are measurably swayed by attributed *expertise/authority* and by the *user's own* assertions.

### Cited Findings
- **Credibility-aware Generation (CAG)** — Pan et al., "Not All Contexts Are Equal: Teaching LLMs Credibility-aware Generation", EMNLP 2024 main, arXiv 2404.06809; attaches a credibility label to each retrieved document and instruction-tunes the model on credibility-guided explanations; code/benchmark/models at github.com/panruotong/CAG — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06809); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.1109.pdf)
- CAG label construction (automatic, not human): relevance = SPLADE retriever score bucketed into high/medium/low; timeliness = gap between query time and publication date downgrades relevance once it exceeds a per-question validity threshold estimated by prompting an LLM three times and voting (formula r_t = max(R − floor(T/threshold), 1)); source reliability = per-scenario fixed levels (e.g., LLM-generated fake news → low, reputable news sites → high); final credibility = min(relevance-time score, source score). Only EvolvingTempQA *answers* are described as manually annotated — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.06809v3)
- CAG benchmark (CAGB) composition: open-domain QA (HotpotQA 500 q / 5,000 docs; 2WikiMHQA 500 / 5,000; MuSiQue 500 / 10,000; ASQA 948 / 4,740; RGB 300 / 11,641), time-sensitive QA (EvolvingTempQA 321 q / 2,247 docs), misinformation-polluted QA (NewsPollutedQA 480 q / 2,400 docs) — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.06809v3)
- CAG results (EM, CAG-13B vs best baseline): HotpotQA 0.514 vs 0.422; 2WikiMHQA 0.604 vs 0.504; MuSiQue 0.408 vs 0.320; ASQA 0.525 vs 0.494; RGB 0.917 vs 0.833; EvolvingTempQA 0.829 vs 0.807; NewsPollutedQA 0.613 (CAG-Mistral-7B) vs 0.430; ~15k training samples — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.06809v3)
- CAG: replacing SPLADE-derived labels with *gold* credibility labels raises CAG-7B average EM from 0.466 to 0.610 on three datasets; authors name annotation precision as the main limit. Simply discarding low-credibility documents underperforms CAG-7B on six datasets; a 0–9 fine-grained relevance scale mostly reduced performance vs 3 levels — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.06809v3)
- **CrAM** — "Credibility-Aware Attention Modification in LLMs for Combating Misinformation in RAG", arXiv 2406.11497: training-free; scales attention in influential heads by document credibility score; reports >20% improvement under misinformation on Qwen1.5-7B **[unverified figure from snippet]** — [arXiv](https://web3.arxiv.org/abs/2406.11497)
- **RA-RAG (Reliability-Aware RAG)** — Hwang et al. (POSTECH), "Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Estimation of Source Reliability", EMNLP 2025 main, arXiv 2410.22954: estimates *source-level* reliability by cross-checking agreement among sources on fact-checking queries (no manual labels), retrieves from top-κ reliable+relevant sources, aggregates by weighted majority voting; argues binary reputation-based credibility "is unsuitable for sources with obscure reputations, and reputation does not necessarily reflect actual reliability" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22954); [ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2025.emnlp-main.1738)
- **"What Evidence Do Language Models Find Convincing?"** — Wan, Wallace, Klein, ACL 2024 (pp. 7468–7484), arXiv 2402.11782: ConflictingQA dataset of controversial queries paired with real web documents that disagree; models weigh *relevance to the query* far more than *style*; adding scientific references or a neutral tone did little or hurt — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11782v2); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.403)
- **"LLMs Trust Humans More, That's a Problem!"** — ACL 2025 long (2025.acl-long.1400): identifies *Authority Bias* — LLMs favour knowledge supplied by the user over retrieved database content even when the user is wrong; tests six LLMs; proposes CDEQ, which performs credibility assessment on conflicting paragraphs **[details from snippet]** — [ACL Anthology PDF](https://preview.aclanthology.org/anlp-naacl/2025.acl-long.1400v1.pdf)
- **"Trust Me, I'm an Expert: Decoding and Steering Authority Bias in LLMs"** — arXiv 2601.13433 (Jan 2026): endorsement text held fixed, attributed source expertise varied across four tiers; susceptibility to *incorrect* endorsements increases with source expertise, with increased confidence in wrong answers; bias is linearly decodable and steerable; limited to smaller open models, math/medical/legal MCQs **[from snippet]** — [arXiv PDF](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2601.13433)
- **AuthorityBench** — arXiv 2603.25092 (Mar 2026): tests whether LLMs can judge which retrieved sources to trust using PageRank-style domain authority and entity popularity; finding: adding web-page text generally *degrades* LLM authority judgment, "indicating authority is not equivalent to textual style, fluency, or narrative richness" **[from snippet]** — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.25092)
- **Astute RAG / MADAM-RAG** (conflict-resolution rather than scoring): "Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Conflicting Evidence", Wang, Prasad, Stengel-Eskin, Bansal, arXiv 2504.13079 (Apr 2025): RAMDocs benchmark (built on AmbigDocs; mixes ambiguity, misinformation, noise; varies how many documents support each valid answer; HF: HanNight/RAMDocs); MADAM-RAG gives each document its own agent, multi-round debate, aggregator; best exact match on RAMDocs still only ~34.4% (Llama3.3-70B) / ~28% (GPT-4o-mini); gains shrink when correct evidence is outnumbered by misinformation **[numbers from snippet]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13079v1); [HF paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2504.13079)

### Inferences
- No published system I found infers *governance status* (active/draft/superseded/secondary summary) as a credibility signal; CAG's three signals (relevance, timeliness, source authority) are the closest and treat "source authority" as a fixed per-site constant. GOV-RAG's status/scope inference would be novel relative to this literature.
- The CAG gold-vs-heuristic gap (0.466 → 0.610) is the best available quantitative argument that investing in accurate governance labels (rather than better prompting) pays off.
- RA-RAG's agreement-based reliability is inappropriate for supersession conflicts: the superseded document and the current one *disagree by design*, and majority agreement would favour whichever version has more copies/summaries in the corpus.
- Wan et al. plus AuthorityBench suggest formal/polished style is *not* what sways models; relevance and explicit authority attributions are. That cuts against the hypothesis "LLMs trust formal-sounding drafts" unless the draft is also the most relevant chunk.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain CrAM's or RA-RAG's exact numeric tables (snippets only).
- I found no paper that *validates automatically derived credibility labels against human judgments* at scale beyond CAG's gold-label ablation.
- "Trust-aware retrieval" as a named line (e.g., TrustRAG) surfaced only as adversarial-robustness work; I did not fetch it and have not confirmed relevance to governance ranking.

## KQ2. Temporal conflicts and recency in RAG: signals, benchmarks, metrics

### Takeaway
2022–2026 produced a dense line of work showing that semantic retrievers rank outdated-but-similar text highly and that LLMs are then distracted by it; fixes add publication/effective-date metadata to retrieval (time-aware encoders, recency filters, version filtering, event graphs). Metrics are mostly accuracy/EM split by "current vs historical" question type, plus date-gated judging (TIDE) and explicit "reject the wrong version" tasks. Importantly, several papers document the *opposite* failure too: recency bias when a historical version actually governs.

### Cited Findings
- **Set the Clock** — Zhao et al., "Set the Clock: Temporal Alignment of Pretrained Language Models", arXiv 2402.16797: LLaMa2 with 2022 cutoff "mostly answer[s] questions using earlier knowledge (e.g., in 2019)"; finetuning to a target year reaches up to 2.8× unaligned performance for 2010 — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.16797)
- **Mitigating Temporal Misalignment by Discarding Outdated Facts** — Zhang & Choi, EMNLP 2023 (2023.emnlp-main.879): predicts how long a fact stays true so models can avoid reciting volatile facts and decide when to seek up-to-date sources — [ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/update-py-deps/2023.emnlp-main.879/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.14824)
- **TemporalWiki** — Jang et al., EMNLP 2022, arXiv 2204.14211: training sets from diffs between consecutive English Wikipedia snapshots (TWiki-Diffsets), probes from Wikidata snapshots labelled UNCHANGED/CHANGED (TWiki-Probes); training on diffs only matched or beat full-snapshot training at 12× less compute; code/data github.com/joeljang/temporalwiki — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.14211); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.418)
- **HoH** — "HoH: A Dynamic Benchmark for Evaluating the Impact of Outdated Information on RAG", arXiv 2503.04800 (v1 Mar 2025, v3 Jul 2025): built with token-level diff algorithms plus LLM pipelines; mock search engine holding both current and historical documents; outdated information (1) reduces accuracy by distracting from correct information, (2) can lead to harmful outputs even when current information is also retrieved; code/data github.com/0russwest0/HoH; an ACL 2025 long version exists (2025.acl-long.301) — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04800); [ACL PDF](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingestion-acl-25/2025.acl-long.301.pdf)
- **ChronoQA** — arXiv 2508.12282 (Aug 2025): Chinese QA benchmark for temporal reasoning in RAG; targets implicit/relative time expressions that cause retrieval of outdated documents **[from snippet]** — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.12282v1)
- **T-GRAG** — arXiv 2508.01680 (Aug 2025): temporally aware GraphRAG for resolving temporal conflicts and redundancy; introduces Time-LongQA built from corporate annual reports **[from snippet]** — [aitopics record](https://aitopics.org/doc/arxivorg:50EA863C)
- **Re3 / Re2-Bench** — "Re3: Relevance & Recency Retrieval for Mitigating Temporal Hallucination", ACL 2026 long (2026.acl-long.1180): time-aware relevance encoder plus a "Conflict-Aware Recency Filter" doing listwise arbitration to suppress obsolete factual versions; benchmark of about 1.3 million items **[size unverified]** — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1180.pdf)
- **Chronos** — "RAG or Learning? Understanding the Limits of LLM Adaptation under Continuous Knowledge Drift", arXiv 2604.05096, Findings of ACL 2026 (2026.findings-acl.546): dynamic event benchmark of time-stamped documents from 2024–2025; vanilla RAG and learning-based methods struggle; proposes a time-aware retrieval baseline organising evidence into an event-evolution graph **[from snippet]** — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.05096); [ACL Findings PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.546.pdf)
- **DeFuzzRAG** — AAAI (paper id 40276): plug-and-play post-retrieval temporal alignment; argues most RAG treats retrieval as purely semantic matching **[from snippet]** — [AAAI PDF](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/40276/44237)
- **DRAGged into Conflicts** — Cattan et al. (Google Research / Bar-Ilan), arXiv 2506.08500 (Jun 2025): CONFLICTS dataset, 458 instances, each with query, retrieved passages, expert-annotated conflict type; *outdated information* = 62 instances (no-conflict 161, complementary 115); expected behaviour for outdated conflicts: "Prioritize up-to-date information, optionally acknowledging outdated sources"; LLMs "often struggle to appropriately resolve conflicts"; data github.com/google-research-datasets/rag_conflicts — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08500v2)
- **ConflictBank** — arXiv 2408.12076: 7,453,853 claim-evidence pairs and 553,117 QA pairs across context-memory, inter-context, intra-memory conflicts including *temporal discrepancies*; largely synthetic — [paper mirror](https://arxiv.deeppaper.ai/papers/2408.12076v1?locale=en)
- **Medical guideline drift**: DriftMedQA / renamed ConflictMedQA, arXiv 2505.07968: current guideline recommendations paired with manually written outdated versions; 7 models on 4,290 scenarios; models "had difficulty rejecting outdated recommendations and frequently endorsed conflicting guidance" — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07968v3). **TempoMed-Bench**, arXiv 2605.13045 (May 2026): 721 MCQs from evolving guideline trajectories; accuracy on historical knowledge is only 25.37%–53.89% of accuracy on up-to-date recommendations **[from snippet]** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.13045)
- Practitioner account of the mechanism (not peer-reviewed): "cosine similarity has no time axis", retired policy clauses can outrank replacements, rank fusion can promote superseded chunks; recommends tracking effective dates separately from ingestion timestamps — [particula.tech blog](https://particula.tech/blog/version-aware-rag-superseded-documents)

### Inferences
- Signals used in this literature: document publication/timestamp, query-time anchor, LLM-estimated fact validity period (CAG), explicit version sequence (VersionRAG, KQ3), token-level diff between versions (HoH, TemporalWiki). Nothing uses *document status words* ("DRAFT", "superseded", "for information only") as a signal — a GOV-RAG differentiator.
- Metrics that transfer directly to GOV-RAG evaluation: (a) accuracy split by current-vs-historical question; (b) TIDE-style hard date gate separating "right content" from "right time"; (c) "detect that the supplied version does not govern" (TIDE: 26.7%), which is the closest published analogue of "refuse the superseded document".
- Recency bias is two-sided: the DRAGged taxonomy *prescribes* "prefer newest", whereas statutory QA (KQ3, "Asking For An Old Friend") shows newest-wins is wrong for historically anchored facts. An evaluation set must therefore include questions where the *older* document governs (e.g., effective-date-anchored queries) to catch naive recency heuristics.

### Gaps
- HoH's dataset size, source corpus and exact degradation figures were not in the abstract; need full PDF.
- Re3's "1.3 million items" and TempoMed-Bench's per-model numbers come from snippets/aggregators only.
- I found no paper measuring *date-aware reranking* in isolation with ablations over date-extraction accuracy.

## KQ3. QA over versioned/superseded documents: policy, legal, regulatory, software docs, standards, contracts

### Takeaway
Three 2025–2026 papers directly target "which version governs": TIDE (Bangladesh customs instruments, 3,050 QA, CC BY 4.0, GitHub), VersionRAG/VersionQA (34 versioned technical documents, 100 questions), and "Asking For An Old Friend" (312 German statutory QA pairs, ICAIL 2026). All three report that version resolution is the hard part and that models under-perform at *rejecting* a non-governing version. Regulatory RAG datasets (ObliQA, ObliQA-MP) exist but do not model supersession; software-versioning benchmarks (VersiCode, CodeUpdateArena) model API-version drift rather than document precedence. No dataset I found covers enterprise HR/IT policy supersession or contracts with amendments.

### Cited Findings
- **TIDE** — Sobhani et al., "Time Present and Time Past: Benchmarking LLMs on Temporally Evolving Document Understanding", arXiv 2608.08512 (v1 9 Aug 2026, v3 25 Aug 2026), CC BY 4.0, https://github.com/icsetepa44/TIDE: 644 official Government of Bangladesh customs instruments (1969–2025; heterogeneous layout, code-mixed, two calendars); 3,050 expert-verified QA pairs across 8 task types; amendments "state what it replaces and when it takes effect"; 9 LLMs under parametric / gold-context / retrieval modes; three-judge LLM council with a hard date gate; best macro accuracy 68.5%; version resolution from implicit date 59.7%; detecting that a supplied version does *not* govern the query 26.7%; models "find correct versions more readily than they reject incorrect ones" and "follow a confident parametric answer over the supplied authoritative text" — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08512); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.08512)
- TIDE's related-work mentions **evolveQA** (timestamped AWS, Azure, WHO updates) as closed-book and lacking any source text stating what a version replaces **[secondary, via TIDE]** — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.08512)
- **VersionRAG / VersionQA** — Huwiler, Stockinger, Fürst, arXiv 2510.08109 (9 Oct 2025): hierarchical graph of version sequences, content boundaries, and inter-version changes; intent-classified retrieval paths; VersionQA = 100 manually curated questions over 34 versioned technical documents; accuracy VersionRAG 90% vs naive RAG 58% vs GraphRAG 64%; implicit change detection 60% vs 0–10% for baselines; 97% fewer indexing tokens than GraphRAG; no code/data URL on the abstract page — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08109v1); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08109v1)
- **Asking For An Old Friend** — Prior, Schultz, Grabmair (TUM), arXiv 2605.23497 (v1 22 May 2026; v2 25 Sep 2026), ICAIL 2026 full paper, Best Paper nominee; 312 expert-validated German statutory QA pairs in three categories (post-cutoff amendment, pre-amendment, multi-provision pre-amendment); five LLMs from OpenAI/Anthropic/DeepSeek; settings Vanilla / Web-search / two RAG variants enforcing temporal validity via fact-date extraction and version filtering; failure modes: *post-cutoff staleness* (applying superseded rules after amendment) and *recency bias* (preferring newer provisions even when a historical version governs); web search "unstable gains and ... marked recency bias on historically anchored tasks"; dataset github.com/TUMLegalTech/temporal_bias **[repo URL from snippet]** — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23497); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23497)
- **LawShift** (Han et al.): legal judgment prediction under statute revisions, 31 types of statutory change **[snippet only]**; **CALRK-Bench**: Korean-law point-in-time selection **[snippet only]** — [arXiv PDF 2603.26332](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.26332)
- **ObliQA / RIRAG (RegNLP)** — arXiv 2409.05677 (Sep 2024, v2 Dec 2024): 27,869 questions over 40 Abu Dhabi Global Markets regulatory documents (~640,000 words); questions generated with GPT-4-turbo-1106 and NLI-filtered; RePASs metric checks obligation coverage and contradiction; **ObliQA-MP** (NLLP 2025, 2025.nllp-1.10) multi-passage extension, 13,191 candidate examples pre-validation; COLING 2025 RIRAG shared task — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05677v2); [HF dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/RegNLP/ObliQA/blob/main/README.md); [NLLP PDF](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-emnlp/2025.nllp-1.10.pdf)
- **VersiCode** — arXiv 2406.07411: 300 libraries, >2,000 versions over nine years; tasks: version-specific code completion and version-aware code editing; most LLMs struggle, especially on newest releases; github.com/wutong8023/VersiCode — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07411v1)
- **CodeUpdateArena** — arXiv 2407.06249: 54 functions from 7 Python packages, 670 examples; synthetic API updates paired with synthesis tasks; knowledge-editing framing — [paper check mirror](https://fugumt.com/fugumt/paper_check/2407.06249v3_enmode)
- **ContraGen** — arXiv 2510.03418 (2025): contradiction-aware benchmark for enterprise RAG using *synthetic* enterprise documents with embedded cross-document contradictions between departments/policies; detect/localize/resolve tasks **[from snippet]** — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.03418v1)
- **EnterpriseRAG-Bench** — arXiv 2605.05253 (v2 May 2026): synthetic company-internal corpus with misfiled documents, near-duplicates, conflicting information **[from snippet]** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05253)
- **WikiContradict** — NeurIPS 2024 D&B, arXiv 2406.13805: 253 human-annotated real Wikipedia contradictions; 5 LLMs, >3,500 human judgments; all models struggle to reflect conflicting context, especially implicit conflicts; automated judge F≈0.8; ibm.biz/wikicontradict — [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/c63819755591ea972f8570beffca6b1b-Abstract.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13805v1)

### Inferences
- TIDE is the only dataset found whose *source documents themselves* carry explicit "replaces X, effective Y" statements, which is exactly GOV-RAG's governance signal. But it is code-mixed Bengali/English customs law; direct reuse is possible (CC BY 4.0), domain transfer is questionable.
- VersionQA (100 q) and the TUM set (312 q) are small; both are suitable as *sanity checks* for GOV-RAG but not for statistically strong claims. Neither has confirmed licence terms for the data.
- ObliQA shows a workable recipe (LLM-generated questions + NLI filtering + obligation-coverage metric) that could be re-run over a corpus with precedence metadata to generate a conflict set in weeks.
- Every enterprise-policy benchmark found (ContraGen, EnterpriseRAG-Bench) is synthetic; a real-corpus supersession benchmark would be a contribution.

### Gaps
- No dataset found for contracts with amendments/addenda, university policy libraries, or tax guidance across years in a QA-under-conflict framing.
- Did not confirm VersionQA document source or licence; abstract page had no repository link.
- "Policy supersession" / "effective date reasoning" as search terms returned no dedicated dataset; TIDE is the nearest.

## KQ4. Public corpora with explicit, machine-readable precedence metadata

### Takeaway
Three corpora are immediately usable with explicit, bulk-downloadable supersession graphs and permissive terms: **IETF RFCs** (9,858 entries; 1,384 with obsoleted-by, 1,246 with updated-by; one XML file), **Python PEPs** (749 PEPs; 31 superseded_by / 35 replaces; one JSON file; CC0/public domain), and **UK legislation.gov.uk** (point-in-time URIs by date; OGL v3). **eCFR** (US) offers date-addressed regulation text via an API with no key. **EUR-Lex** consolidated texts and **W3C** Superseded/Obsolete Recommendations have the relations but are harder to extract in bulk; **ISO** is proprietary. None of these corpora has, to my knowledge, been used for QA-under-conflict in a published paper (TIDE/TUM use other legal corpora).

### Cited Findings

**IETF RFC index**

Per-entry elements include doc-id, title, author, date(month/year), format, page-count, keywords, abstract, draft, obsoletes, obsoleted-by, updates, updated-by, current-status, publication-status, stream, area, doi, errata-url. Other entry types: bcp-entry 247, std-entry 93, fyi-entry 36, rfc-not-issued-entry 189 **[computed locally 2026-11-14]**
- Counts: 9,858 rfc-entry records; highest RFC number 10082; entries with obsoletes 1,265; obsoleted-by 1,384; updates 1,296; updated-by 1,246; errata-url 2,488. current-status: PROPOSED STANDARD 4,438; INFORMATIONAL 3,013; UNKNOWN 887; EXPERIMENTAL 559; HISTORIC 353; BEST CURRENT PRACTICE 337; DRAFT STANDARD 138; INTERNET STANDARD 133. RFCs per year: 2022 194, 2023 173, 2024 175, 2025 208, 2026 (to date) 156. Example: RFC2616 obsoleted-by RFC7230–RFC7235, current-status DRAFT STANDARD **[computed locally 2026-11-14]**
- Header semantics: RFC 7322 (RFC Style Guide) §4.1.4 "Updates and Obsoletes": "When an RFC obsoletes or updates a previously published RFC or RFCs, this information is included in the document header"; formats "Updates: nnnn, ..., nnnn" / "Obsoletes: nnnn, ..., nnnn", listed in ascending order; RFC 7322 gives no formal definition of the two relations — [RFC 7322](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7322.html)

**UK legislation (legislation.gov.uk)**
- Point-in-time URIs: append YYYY-MM-DD to a document or section URI (e.g., /ukpga/1998/29/section/1/2010-04-01) to get the version in force on that date; omit for current; `enacted`/`made`/`created` for original; `prospective` for all planned amendments applied; dates before base date 1 Feb 1991 redirect to that base version; pre-enactment dates return 404; not-yet-in-force sections are flagged — [legislation.gov.uk URIs](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/developer/uris)
- Formats: XML via a Legislation Schema (Dublin Core metadata, XHTML tables, MathML), representation URIs `.../data.xml`, content negotiation — [legislation.gov.uk formats](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/developer/formats)
- Licence: Open Government Licence v3.0 except where stated; EUR-Lex-derived content reused under Commission Decision 2011/833/EU — [legislation.gov.uk formats](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/developer/formats)
- Item count: not stated on the developer pages (gap).

**US Code (Office of the Law Revision Counsel)**
- Bulk XML (USLM schema), XHTML, PDF; whole Code or per title; current release point "Public Law 119-118 (September 30, 2026)"; USLM published since July 2013; moving to USLM v2; PCC format discontinued; prior release points "not yet available on this site" — [uscode.house.gov download](https://uscode.house.gov/download/download.shtml); [annual historical archives](https://uscode.house.gov/download/annualhistoricalarchives/downloadxhtml.shtml)
- GovInfo bulk data repository lists Federal Register (2000–present), eCFR, and US Code — [govinfo developers](https://govinfo.gov/developers)
- Public-domain status of US federal works: not fetched here **[assumed under 17 U.S.C. §105; unverified in this session]**

### Inferences
- **Fastest 2–3-week path**: RFC index + PEP index. Both are single-file downloads, both encode supersession as explicit graph edges (obsoletes/obsoleted-by/updates/updated-by; replaces/superseded_by), both have status fields that map onto GOV-RAG's active/draft/superseded taxonomy (RFC: PROPOSED/INTERNET STANDARD vs HISTORIC vs Internet-Drafts; PEP: Final/Active vs Draft vs Superseded/Withdrawn/Rejected). The RFC "updates" relation (partial amendment, both documents remain active) is a natural test of *scope-of-applicability* rather than wholesale replacement. Internet-Drafts (datatracker) provide genuine "draft vs published" distractors.
- Ground-truth construction: a question whose answer changed between RFC A and its obsoleting RFC B (or PEP A → PEP B) has a determinable governing document; distractor retrieval of the obsoleted text is exactly the failure GOV-RAG targets. Thousands of candidate pairs exist (1,384 obsoleted RFCs).
- Legislation.gov.uk and eCFR provide *date-anchored* ground truth, enabling "older version governs for this date" questions that catch recency-only heuristics. Terms are permissive (OGL v3; US federal).
- EUR-Lex is rich (ELI "amends"/"is consolidated by") but needs SPARQL work; W3C has clear status semantics but an uncertain bulk listing; ISO is unusable for text.

- IETF TLP 5.0 clause text (reproduction/derivative rights for RFC text) not read; verify before redistributing an RFC-derived dataset.
- No count of EUR-Lex consolidated texts, W3C Superseded Recommendations, or UK legislation items was located.
- Federal Register API field list (effective_on, cfr_references, etc.) unverified (site blocked the fetch).
- SEC rule updates, university policy libraries, tax guidance across years: no public corpus with machine-readable supersession found; not searched exhaustively.
- No published paper found that uses RFCs, PEPs, EUR-Lex, US Code, eCFR, W3C or ISO as a QA-under-conflict testbed.

## KQ5. Known LLM failure modes relevant to "which document governs"

### Takeaway
The literature documents: context bias (accepting wrong context) and prior bias (ignoring correct context) as a two-sided tug-of-war; majority/frequency bias and confirmation bias when several documents conflict; recency bias in web-search settings even when an older provision governs; authority bias toward user-supplied or expert-attributed claims; poor ability to *reject* a non-governing version; and weak sensitivity to stylistic polish. No study was found that isolates "newest-but-draft" or "ignores scope restriction" as named failure modes.

### Cited Findings
- **Context vs prior bias (ClashEval)** — NeurIPS 2024 D&B, arXiv 2404.10198: >1,200 questions across six domains (drug dosages, Olympic records, news, names, locations, dates); perturbs reference documents; LLMs "exhibit a significant context bias, often overriding their correct internal prior knowledge when presented with incorrect external information"; measures both prior-biased and context-biased errors; notes that a benchmark measuring only rejection of wrong context can be gamed by always ignoring context — [NeurIPS PDF](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/3aa291abc426d7a29fb08418c1244177-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.10198v2)
- **Receptiveness + confirmation bias** — Xie et al., "Adaptive Chameleon or Stubborn Sloth", ICLR 2024 Spotlight, arXiv 2305.13300: LLMs are "highly receptive to external evidence even when that conflicts with their parametric memory" if coherent; show "strong confirmation bias when the external evidence contains some information that is consistent" with memory — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13300)
- **Majority rule, Dunning-Kruger, availability bias** — Jin et al., "Tug-of-War Between Knowledge", LREC-COLING 2024, arXiv 2402.14409: models lean toward "evidence that appears more frequently" (majority rule); stronger models "persistently favoring their faulty internal memory even when correct evidence is provided"; availability bias toward common knowledge; proposes CD2 contrastive decoding — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14409)
- **Recency bias when an older version governs** — "Asking For An Old Friend", ICAIL 2026: web search "exhibits a marked recency bias on historically anchored tasks"; models apply superseded provisions post-amendment and prefer newer provisions when a historical version governs — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23497)
- **Failure to reject the wrong version** — TIDE: 26.7% at detecting that a supplied version does not govern vs 59.7% at resolving the right version from an implicit date; models follow confident parametric answers over supplied authoritative text — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08512)
- **Outdated info harms even when current info present** — HoH abstract — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04800); DriftMedQA/ConflictMedQA: models "frequently endorsed conflicting guidance" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07968v3)
- **Authority bias toward the user** — ACL 2025 "LLMs Trust Humans More, That's a Problem!" — [ACL PDF](https://preview.aclanthology.org/anlp-naacl/2025.acl-long.1400v1.pdf); **toward attributed experts** — arXiv 2601.13433 — [arXiv PDF](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2601.13433)
- **Style is not what convinces** — Wan et al. ACL 2024: relevance dominates; references/neutral tone edits did little or hurt — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11782v2); AuthorityBench: page text degrades authority judgments, "authority is not equivalent to textual style, fluency, or narrative richness" **[snippet]** — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.25092)
- **Small-minority correct evidence** — RAMDocs/MADAM-RAG: gains shrink when supporting evidence is heavily outnumbered by misinformation — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13079v1)
- **Conflict-type-specific expected behaviour** — DRAGged into Conflicts prescribes "prioritize up-to-date information" for outdated-type conflicts and finds explicit prompting of conflict type helps — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08500v2)
- Practitioner reports (not peer-reviewed) of outdated HR policy outranking its replacement due to wording density and chunk dilution — [dev.to](https://dev.to/alaikrm/when-rag-gives-wrong-answers-a-debugging-walkthrough-2bn8); of QA models answering from whichever conflicting policy they attended to most, with no warning — [Towards Data Science](https://towardsdatascience.com/your-rag-system-retrieves-the-right-data-but-still-produces-wrong-answers-heres-why-and-how-to-fix-it/)

### Inferences
- Majority/frequency bias (Jin et al.) predicts a specific GOV-RAG hazard: secondary summaries, FAQs and copies of an *old* policy can outvote a single current authoritative document. A corpus with many derivative documents (RFC-citing blog posts; policy summaries) is needed to test this.
- The "newest-even-if-draft" hypothesis is plausible from recency-bias findings but *not* directly tested anywhere I found; Internet-Drafts vs RFCs, or PEP Draft vs Final, give a natural, labelled testbed for it.
- "Ignores scope restrictions" is untested; RFC "Updates" (partial, scoped amendments) and EUR-Lex per-passage provenance tags are the best labelled material to construct scope-conflict questions.
- Because ClashEval shows evaluation can be gamed by always ignoring (or always trusting) context, a GOV-RAG eval needs balanced cases: newest governs / older governs / draft must be ignored / secondary summary must be ignored / scoped update applies only to subset.

### Gaps
- No paper found that names "draft preference" or "scope-restriction blindness" as a failure mode or measures it.
- Order/position effects among conflicting documents were not confirmed (the Tug-of-War abstract did not cover truncation/order).
- The Knowledge Conflicts survey (Xu et al., arXiv 2403.08319) abstract gives only the taxonomy (context-memory, inter-context, intra-memory); its detailed inter-context findings were not extracted — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08319); [GitHub list](https://github.com/pillowsofwind/Knowledge-Conflicts-Survey)
