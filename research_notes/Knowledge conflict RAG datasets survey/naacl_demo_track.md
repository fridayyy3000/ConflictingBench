# NAACL 2027 System Demonstrations track and what makes a strong *ACL RAG/QA demo paper

Research date: 14 November 2026. Everything in the "NAACL 2027 CFP" section below is CONFIRMED from the official 2027 call page (fetched today). Prior-year CFPs (NAACL 2024/2025, ACL 2025/2026) are included to show how requirements have drifted and to infer reviewer expectations. Acceptance statistics come from the demo-track proceedings prefaces (ACL Anthology front matter PDFs, text-extracted locally) and ACL demo-chair business reports.

## Key Question 1: NAACL 2027 System Demonstrations CFP — exact requirements

### Takeaway
The NAACL 2027 demo CFP is published (announced 12 Sep 2026). Deadline is **4 December 2026, 11:59 pm UTC-12 (AoE)**, via OpenReview, single-blind, 6 pages + 2-page appendix, a **required** screencast video of up to 2.5 minutes, a **required** live-demo or download link (desk reject otherwise), and a **required** evaluation of some form (desk reject otherwise). ARR is not involved; the demo track is reviewed separately and has no rebuttal. Notification 10 Feb 2027, camera-ready 3 Mar 2027, conference 1–5 June 2027 in San Francisco.

**Format and policy (confirmed for 2027)**
- Page limit: 6 pages of content; unlimited extra space for ethics/broader-impact statement and references; **appendices limited to 2 pages**; accepted papers get one additional page to address reviewer feedback — [NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Anonymity: **single-blind** — "the submission should not be anonymous, but the reviewers will be anonymous"; self-references allowed — [NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Demo video: a screencast of **up to 2.5 minutes is required**; used for evaluation and not published unless requested; narrated video preferred; "Production quality is not a major concern" — [NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Live demo / download link: **required**; "submissions without one are desk rejected"; the only exception is when a link is "clearly impossible (e.g., when specific hardware is required)," and authors must explain why — [NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Evaluation: **"submissions that fail to provide any form of evaluation will be desk rejected."** The CFP says strong submissions show user studies, real-world deployment, systematic comparison to baselines, or measurable novel contributions, and that submissions that mainly wrap existing APIs or assemble off-the-shelf models without substantive contribution will not be accepted (paraphrased from the call) — [NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Open source: not required; the call welcomes open-source contributors and **requires a license statement** (the paper must answer "How is the system licensed?") — [NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Review criteria: reviewers assess "validity, quality, and relevance to natural language processing" and are expected to interact with the demo; the Best Demo Award is judged on "innovation, the contribution and the completeness of the live demo system" — [NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- ARR: not used for demos; papers under review at NAACL cannot be simultaneously submitted elsewhere, "including the ARR" — [NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Overlap: no significant overlap (>25%) in content or results with other papers under review or published; violations desk-rejected — [NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Presentation: at least one author must present a live demo at a demo session at NAACL 2027 with an accompanying poster; the program is not for commercial sales/marketing (that belongs in the Exhibit Program) — [NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Generative AI disclosure: any use of generative AI to build the demo or write the paper must be disclosed in the Acknowledgements — [NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Ethics: authors must follow the ACM Code of Ethics; papers on sensitive data/tasks must discuss ethical issues; conditional acceptances are re-reviewed by an ethics committee — [NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Style: the ACL 2026 and 2025 demo calls say to use the main-conference style files and that papers "that do not follow the official style guidelines and page limits will be automatically rejected" — [ACL 2026 demo CFP](https://2026.aclweb.org/calls/system_demonstration/); [NAACL 2025 demo CFP](https://2025.naacl.org/calls/demo/)

**Year-over-year comparison (how NAACL demo CFPs changed)**
- NAACL 2024: deadline Jan 22, 2024; notification Mar 20, 2024; camera-ready Apr 1, 2024; 6 pages; video up to **2 minutes required**; live demo/download link only **"strongly recommended"**; **no stated evaluation requirement**; criteria "relevance to the demo track, contribution, clarity, completeness, and novelty"; >30% improvement rule for previously reported systems; chairs Nazneen Rajani, Kai-Wei Chang, Annie Lee — [NAACL 2024 demo CFP](https://2024.naacl.org/calls/demos/)
- NAACL 2025: deadline Dec 15, 2024; notification listed as both Feb 24 and Feb 28, 2025; camera-ready Mar 7, 2025; 6 pages, **unlimited appendix**; video up to **2 minutes**; live link **"strongly recommended"**; criteria "relevance to the demo track, contribution, clarity, completeness, and novelty"; >30% improvement rule; chairs Nouha Dziri, Sean (Xiang) Ren, Shizhe Diao — [NAACL 2025 demo CFP](https://2025.naacl.org/calls/demo/)
- NAACL 2027 (vs. 2025): video lengthened to **2.5 min**; live link moved from recommended to **required with desk-reject**; evaluation moved from unstated to **required with desk-reject**; appendix capped at **2 pages**; license statement required; generative-AI disclosure added; ACM Code of Ethics replaces ACL/ARR ethics references — [NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/) vs. [NAACL 2025 demo CFP](https://2025.naacl.org/calls/demo/)
- The NAACL 2027 wording tracks the ACL 2025/2026 demo CFPs almost exactly (6 pages + 2-page appendix, 2.5-min video, desk reject for no link or no evaluation, single-blind, no rebuttal, 25% overlap rule) — [ACL 2025 demo CFP](https://2025.aclweb.org/calls/system_demonstration/); [ACL 2026 demo CFP](https://2026.aclweb.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- ACL 2025/2026 CFPs list the questions a demo paper should answer: "What problem does the system solve? Why is the system important, and what impact does it have? What is novel about the system's approach or technology? Who is the target audience? How does the system work? How does it compare with existing systems? How is the system licensed? How was the system evaluated, and were user studies or human evaluations conducted?" — [ACL 2026 demo CFP](https://2026.aclweb.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Third-party aggregators (e.g., mlscientist.com, educutai.com) restate the Dec 4 deadline, 6 pages, 2.5-min video, and live-link requirement; they are not authoritative but are consistent with the official page — [mlscientist NAACL 2027 demo call](https://mlscientist.com/naacl-2027-demo-call-united-states/)

### Inferences
- A team with a FastAPI backend + web UI must publish a reachable live URL (or a downloadable, installable package) before Dec 4; this is a hard gate, not a nice-to-have. The URL must survive through the review window (Dec–Feb) because reviewers "are expected to interact with the demo."
- Because the track is single-blind, prior arXiv or GitHub exposure of the system is not a problem, but the >25% overlap rule means any companion research paper (e.g., an ARR submission on the same method) must be clearly distinct in content and results.
- "Any form of evaluation" is the floor; the CFP's own list of what strong submissions do (user studies, deployment, baseline comparisons, measurable novel contribution) is effectively the rubric. A 30-question synthetic benchmark alone satisfies the desk-reject gate but is on the weak end; see Key Question 2.
- The 2-page appendix cap means extended benchmark tables, prompts and screenshots need to be budgeted carefully; prior demos used unlimited appendices.

### Gaps
- The official page does not publish the OpenReview review form fields or numeric score scales for 2027.
- Whether NAACL 2027 demo papers will appear in a separate "System Demonstrations" companion volume is not stated on the 2027 page (it has been the practice every prior year, so presumably yes).
- The 2027 page does not say whether the 2.5-minute video must be narrated by a human; the ACL 2025 chairs recommended banning text-to-speech narration (see KQ2), so assume human narration is safest.

## Key Question 2: What demo-track reviewers reward and penalise; acceptance statistics

### Takeaway
Demo tracks accept roughly a third of submissions (34–38% at ACL/EMNLP 2024–2026; 43% at the small NAACL 2024 track). Chairs reward a working, publicly reachable system with a clear NLP contribution, visible evaluation, visual aids (screenshots/diagrams), and a narrated video; they desk-reject for missing link, missing evaluation, over-length, >25% overlap, and penalise API-wrapper systems, commercial pitches, text-to-speech/silent videos and missing required materials.

**Acceptance statistics (from official prefaces and chair reports)**

- ACL 2024 demo track: 114 submissions, 108 valid with required materials, 38 accepted, 35%; reviewer pool of 178 (161 assigned), 88% of reviews on time, >30 emergency reviewers — [2024.acl-demos.0 front matter](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-demos.0.pdf); [ACL 2024Q3 Demo Chairs report](https://www.aclweb.org/adminwiki/index.php/2024Q3_Reports:_Demo_Chairs)

**What chairs/CFPs say is rewarded**
- Every track preface repeats: "papers describing system demonstrations, ranging from early prototypes to mature, production-ready systems. Publicly available open-source or open-access systems are of special interest" — [2026.acl-demo.0](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-demo.0.pdf); [2024.naacl-demo.0](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-demo.0.pdf)
- Review criteria used by NAACL 2024/2025: "relevance to the demo track, contribution, clarity, completeness, and novelty" — [NAACL 2025 demo CFP](https://2025.naacl.org/calls/demo/)
- Best demo criteria (ACL 2026, NAACL 2027): "the innovation, the contribution and the completeness of the live demo system"; ACL 2025 states the award "is for the best demo, not the best paper" and a committee interacts with the demo — [ACL 2026 demo CFP](https://2026.aclweb.org/calls/system_demonstration/); [ACL 2025 demo CFP](https://2025.aclweb.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- ACL 2024 and 2025 chairs explicitly "encourage visual aids such as screenshots or diagrams" — [ACL 2024Q3 Demo Chairs report](https://www.aclweb.org/adminwiki/index.php/2024Q3_Reports:_Demo_Chairs); [ACL 2025Q3 Demo Chairs report](https://www.aclweb.org/adminwiki/index.php/2025Q3_Reports:_Demo_Chairs)
- ACL demo chair handbook: each submission gets 3 expert reviewers; chairs encourage reviewer discussion for borderline cases; final decisions are "based on reviewer recommendations and the number of demo slots" and aim to balance topic areas; best demo chosen from reviewer scores and AC nominations — [ACL Demonstration Chair Handbook 2025](https://acl-org.github.io/conference-handbook/2025/demo)

**What is penalised / desk-rejected**
- No live link or installable package (ACL 2025/2026, NAACL 2027): desk reject — [ACL 2025 demo CFP](https://2025.aclweb.org/calls/system_demonstration/); [NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- No evaluation: "may be desk rejected" (ACL 2025/2026), "will be desk rejected" (NAACL 2027) — [ACL 2026 demo CFP](https://2026.aclweb.org/calls/system_demonstration/); [NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Over 6 pages / appendix over 2 pages / wrong style: desk reject — [ACL 2026 demo CFP](https://2026.aclweb.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- >25% overlap with other submissions; same work to ARR and demo track → rejected by all tracks — [NAACL 2025 demo CFP](https://2025.naacl.org/calls/demo/)
- Previously reported system without >30% improvement (NAACL 2024/2025 rule) — [NAACL 2024 demo CFP](https://2024.naacl.org/calls/demos/)
- Systems that mainly wrap existing APIs or assemble off-the-shelf models without substantive contribution "will not be accepted" (NAACL 2027) — [NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Commercial sales/marketing content is "not appropriate" — [ACL 2025 demo CFP](https://2025.aclweb.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Missing required materials: 6 of 114 (ACL 2024), 9 of 187 (ACL 2025), 12 of 227 (ACL 2026) submissions were invalid for lacking required materials — [2024.acl-demos.0](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-demos.0.pdf); [2025.acl-demo.0](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-demo.0.pdf); [2026.acl-demo.0](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-demo.0.pdf)
- Video quality problem flagged by ACL 2025 chairs: "several submitted videos were done using text-to-speech or no voice over"; they recommend prohibiting TTS narration and silent videos in future — [ACL 2025Q3 Demo Chairs report](https://www.aclweb.org/adminwiki/index.php/2025Q3_Reports:_Demo_Chairs)
- Papers on sensitive data/tasks that do not discuss ethical issues "will not be accepted"; ethics-flagged papers go to an ethics committee — [ACL 2025 demo CFP](https://2025.aclweb.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- General ACL reviewing guidance (not demo-specific): reviewers should weigh "the contributions of the paper" and "what impact it may have on our community"; unclear research question is a common weakness — [ACL 2021 Reviewing Advice](https://2021.aclweb.org/blog/reviewing-advice/)
- Analogous demo-track framing from data management (SIGMOD Record): demos are judged on "system novelty, interactivity, usability, and demonstration feasibility" and "clarity, technical contribution, and practical feasibility" — [SIGMOD Record Open Forum, Bhowmick](https://sigmodrecord.org/publications/sigmodRecord/2606/pdfs/11_OpenForum_Bhowmick.pdf)

### Inferences
- With ~35% acceptance and 3 reviewers who actually open the demo, the most common failure modes are operational (link down, video unclear, missing materials) rather than scientific; the invalid-submission counts show 5–8% of submitters lose before review for missing materials.
- The explicit NAACL 2027 language against "wrapping existing APIs" is directly relevant to an LLM-backed RAG prototype: the paper must foreground what is novel in the conflict-handling logic and the evidence-inspection UI, not the LLM/vector-DB plumbing.
- Reviewers for RAG demos in 2024–2026 accepted systems whose evaluation was modest (efficiency numbers only, e.g., RAGViz) but every accepted paper surveyed had at least a table of numbers or a human/expert evaluation; a synthetic 30-question benchmark plus a small expert or user study would put the submission in the typical range.

### Gaps
- No public reviewer scoring form/rubric for the NAACL 2027 demo track was found; ACL demo reviews are not public on OpenReview. The only concrete criteria lists are from the CFPs above.
- NAACL 2025 demo-track submission count and acceptance rate were not found in any official source (the front matter has no preface; no chair business report surfaced).
- No blog posts or tweets from NAACL/ACL demo chairs explaining decisions were found; the chair business reports on the ACL admin wiki are the closest primary source.

## Key Question 3: Accepted RAG / QA / fact-checking / evidence demo papers (2024–2026) to model on

### Takeaway
Across the six demo volumes surveyed (NAACL 2024/2025, ACL 2024/2025/2026, EMNLP 2024/2025) there are roughly 40 RAG/QA/attribution/fact-checking demos. The closest analogues to a conflict-aware, evidence-inspectable RAG system are **VeraCT Scan** (ACL 2024; corroborating vs conflicting reports weighted by source credibility), **FACTS&EVIDENCE** (NAACL 2025; per-claim credibility with diverse evidence attribution), **PRAISE** (ACL 2025; contradictions between two sources shown with evidence), **TruthReader** (EMNLP 2024; inline citations to document paragraphs), **InspectorRAGet** (NAACL 2025; RAG output introspection), **KMatrix-2** (EMNLP 2025; explicit inter-context and context-memory knowledge-conflict resolution), and **PledgeTracker** (EMNLP 2025; evaluated with professional fact-checkers). Nearly all provide GitHub code and a video; most are 6–7 content pages plus references/appendix; evaluations range from efficiency-only to benchmark + expert study.

### Cited Findings
Per-paper notes. "PDF pages" = full Anthology PDF including references/appendix; "content pages" = page on which the References heading first appears (so content ≈ that page or the one before). Figure/table counts are from captions in the extracted text.

1. **VeraCT Scan: Retrieval-Augmented Fake News Detection with Justifiable Reasoning** — Cheng Niu et al., ACL 2024 demos (2024.acl-demos.25). Extracts core facts, searches the web "to identify corroborating or conflicting reports," then "sources' credibility is leveraged for information verification"; provides rationale per supporting/conflicting judgment; uses mediabiasfactcheck.com and NewsBreak credibility ratings; GPT-4 Turbo plus fine-tuned Llama-2 13B. Evaluation: benchmark numbers on several fake-news datasets (accuracy/precision/recall/F1, micro-F1 for fact verification); no user study. GitHub + video present. 12 PDF pages, references from p.7, 1 figure, 9 tables. Sections: Intro, Related Work, Approach (incl. "Source Credibility and Final Decision"), Experimental Settings, Results, Conclusion, Limitations, Ethics — [ACL Anthology 2024.acl-demos.25](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-demos.25.pdf)
2. **FACTS&EVIDENCE: An Interactive Tool for Transparent Fine-Grained Factual Verification of Machine-Generated Text** — Varich Boonsanong et al., NAACL 2025 demos (2025.naacl-demo.35, pp. 437–448). Breaks text into claims, visualises per-claim credibility, explains decisions and attributes to "multiple, diverse evidence sources"; aims to let users "selectively trust" text. Evaluation: FAVA dataset, "competitive with strong baselines"; user-study terms present. GitHub + video present. 12 PDF pages, references from p.7, 5 figures, 6 tables — [ACL Anthology 2025.naacl-demo.35](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-demo.35.pdf)
3. **PRAISE: Enhancing Product Descriptions with LLM-Driven Structured Insights** — Adnan Qidwai et al., ACL 2025 demos (2025.acl-demo.62). Interface "to identify missing, contradictory, or partially matching details between these two sources" (seller description vs. reviews) "alongside supporting evidence." Evaluation: results/error analysis, baseline and ablation analysis, model comparison; user-study terms present. Project page, demo video and "Try It Out" link; no GitHub found in text. 9 PDF pages, references from p.8, 9 figures (screenshots), 1 table — [ACL Anthology 2025.acl-demo.62](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-demo.62.pdf)
4. **TruthReader: Towards Trustworthy Document Assistant Chatbot with Reliable Attribution** — Dongfang Li et al., EMNLP 2024 demos (2024.emnlp-demo.10). Open-source document chatbot generating "detailed inline citations, which can be attributed to the original document paragraphs"; refuses unanswerable questions. Evaluation: retrieval benchmark (Recall@1–4, 1,919 instances) and generation table reporting Answer Accuracy, Refusal Recall, Citation Precision vs Claude-3-Opus/GPT-4/Mixtral; no user study. GitHub, HF models, YouTube video. 12 PDF pages, references from p.8, 5 figures, 6 tables. Sections: Intro, User Interface, System Architecture, Implementation, Evaluation, Related Work, Conclusion — [ACL Anthology 2024.emnlp-demo.10](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-demo.10.pdf)
5. **InspectorRAGet: An Introspection Platform for RAG Evaluation** — Kshitij P. Fadnis et al. (IBM), NAACL 2025 demos (2025.naacl-demo.13, pp. 125–134). Analyses aggregate and instance-level RAG output quality with human and algorithmic metrics and annotator quality; live instance at ibm.biz/InspectorRAGet. Evaluation: case studies ("Analyzing RAG Model Performance", "Analyzing LLM-as-a-Judge Performance"); no benchmark table. GitHub present; no video link found in text. 10 PDF pages, references from p.7, 2 figures, 1 table — [ACL Anthology 2025.naacl-demo.13](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-demo.13.pdf)
6. **KMatrix-2: A Comprehensive Heterogeneous Knowledge Collaborative Enhancement Toolkit for LLMs** — Shun Wu et al., EMNLP 2025 demos (2025.emnlp-demos.42). Toolkit offering "systematic inter-context and context-memory knowledge conflict resolution methods" for knowledge-enhanced LLMs. Evaluation: benchmark experiments (RuleBench etc.), no user study. GitHub + video. 11 PDF pages, references from p.7, 6 figures, 7 tables — [ACL Anthology 2025.emnlp-demos.42](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-demos.42.pdf)
7. **PledgeTracker: A System for Monitoring the Fulfilment of Pledges** — Yulong Chen et al., EMNLP 2025 demos (2025.emnlp-demos.64). Multi-step evidence retrieval + timeline construction + fulfilment filter. Evaluation: offline (fulfilment filtering P/R/F1) and real-world use with Full Fact fact-checkers, 12 Jun–8 Sep 2025, 68 pledges, 513 events judged; 0.78 URL-level F1 vs Google Search 0.23 and GPT-4o web_search 0.03. 12 PDF pages, references from p.8, 4 figures, 4 tables. No GitHub/video found in text — [ACL Anthology 2025.emnlp-demos.64](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-demos.64.pdf)
8. **Medico: Towards Hallucination Detection and Correction with Multi-source Evidence Fusion** — Xinping Zhao et al., EMNLP 2024 demos (2024.emnlp-demo.4). Fuses evidence from web, KB and KG; detects, explains and revises hallucinations. Evaluation: HaluEval — retrieval 0.964 HR@5 / 0.908 MRR@5, detection F1 0.927–0.951, correction approval 0.973–0.979; per-source vs fused table; no user study. GitHub + YouTube video. 12 PDF pages, 3 figures, 6 tables — [ACL Anthology 2024.emnlp-demo.4](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-demo.4.pdf)
9. **Ai2 Scholar QA: Organized Literature Synthesis with Attribution** — Amanpreet Singh et al., ACL 2025 demos (2025.acl-demo.49). Free online scientific QA with open-source pipeline (qa.allen.ai). Evaluation: retrieval tuning on 500 synthetic queries, reranking and generation experiments, outperforms competing systems on a scientific QA benchmark, plus real-world usage feedback ("85% of user feedback is positive"). GitHub, YouTube video, Python package. 11 PDF pages, references from p.8, 8 figures, 6 tables. Sections: Intro, Pipeline, Interface and Source Code, Evaluation (incl. "Real-world Usage and User Feedback"), Related Work, Conclusion — [ACL Anthology 2025.acl-demo.49](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-demo.49.pdf)
10. **CiteLab: Developing and Diagnosing LLM Citation Generation Workflows via the Human-LLM Interaction** — Jiajun Shen et al., ACL 2025 demos (2025.acl-demo.47). Modular citation-generation framework (Citeflow) + visual interface (Citefix); evaluation via a use case with baselines and "multiple rounds of improvement"; GitHub present. 12 PDF pages, references from p.7, 6 figures, 3 tables — [ACL Anthology 2025.acl-demo.47](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-demo.47.pdf)
11. **LCDS: A Logic-Controlled Discharge Summary Generation System Supporting Source Attribution and Expert Review** — Cheng Yuan et al., ACL 2025 demos (2025.acl-demo.28). Source-mapping table for attribution and expert review loop; evaluation section with accuracy/coherence claims and expert review; GitHub repo includes demo video. 11 PDF pages, references from p.7, 2 figures, 2 tables — [ACL Anthology 2025.acl-demo.28](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-demo.28.pdf)
12. **RAGViz: Diagnose and Visualize Retrieval-Augmented Generation** — Tevin Wang et al., EMNLP 2024 demos (2024.emnlp-demo.33). Token/document attention visualisation and generation comparison on adding/removing context docs. Evaluation: efficiency only (median query ~5 s; snippeting technique latency/relevance trade-off); no accuracy benchmark. Open-source, video present, license stated. 8 PDF pages, 5 figures, 3 tables; sections: Intro, Features and Use Cases, System Architecture, Experiment, Conclusion, Limitations — [ACL Anthology 2024.emnlp-demo.33](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-demo.33.pdf)

### Inferences
- No accepted demo paper found in 2024–2026 is squarely about *conflict-aware RAG for enterprise/policy QA*; the nearest are VeraCT Scan (conflicting web reports + source credibility, news domain), PRAISE (two-source contradictions), FACTS&EVIDENCE (per-claim trust with multiple sources) and KMatrix-2 (toolkit-level conflict resolution). A demo that surfaces conflicting evidence to the user and lets them inspect/resolve it would occupy an open niche; the paper should cite these four as the closest prior demos and position against them.
- The common evaluation pattern among accepted RAG/QA demos is "component benchmark table(s) + one of {expert/user study, deployment feedback, baseline/ablation}." Efficiency-only (RAGViz) and case-study-only (InspectorRAGet) evaluations were accepted in 2024/2025 but those tracks did not yet have the mandatory-evaluation rule.
- Almost every accepted paper surveyed had a GitHub repo and a video; about half stated a license explicitly. Only two (PledgeTracker, My Climate CoPilot) lacked an obvious code link in the text, and both compensated with strong expert evaluations.

- Full-text review of the ~20 additional titles listed by ID was not done; their evaluation type is unknown.

## Key Question 4: Typical structure and length of an accepted demo paper

### Takeaway
Accepted RAG/QA demos run 6–7 content pages (6 at submission + 1 camera-ready page) followed by references and a 1–8 page appendix (unlimited in 2024–2025; now capped at 2 pages for ACL 2025+/NAACL 2027). Typical skeleton: Introduction (problem + contributions) → User Interface / Usage Example (screenshot-heavy) → System Architecture / Implementation → Evaluation (1–1.5 pages) → Related Work → Conclusion (+ Limitations, Ethics). Median paper has about 5 figures (mostly UI screenshots and an architecture diagram) and 3–6 tables.

### Cited Findings
- Camera-ready length rule: 6 pages + "one additional page" for accepted papers, unlimited references/ethics, appendix ≤2 pages — [NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Observed total PDF lengths across 21 accepted RAG/QA demos: 7 (FactSearch), 8 (RAGViz), 9 (DOCMASTER, My Climate CoPilot, PRAISE), 10 (InspectorRAGet), 11 (OpenFactCheck, LCDS, Ai2 Scholar QA, KMatrix-2, ConfReady), 12 (TruthReader, Medico, CiteLab, FACTS&EVIDENCE, VeraCT Scan, PledgeTracker), 14 (ELLA), 15 (Marcel), 16 (SpiritRAG, LocalRQA); the References heading appears on page 6–8 in every case, i.e. 6–7 content pages — extracted from the Anthology PDFs cited in KQ3
- Figure counts observed: 1 (VeraCT), 2 (InspectorRAGet, LCDS), 3 (Medico, LocalRQA, FactSearch), 4 (OpenFactCheck, ELLA, My Climate CoPilot, PledgeTracker), 5 (TruthReader, RAGViz, DOCMASTER, FACTS&EVIDENCE), 6 (CiteLab, Marcel, KMatrix-2, ConfReady), 8 (Ai2 Scholar QA), 9 (SpiritRAG, PRAISE) — extracted from the Anthology PDFs cited in KQ3
- Representative section outlines: TruthReader — "1 Introduction; 2 User Interface; 3 System Architecture; 4 Implementation; 5 Evaluation (5.1 Conversational Document Retrieval Benchmark, 5.2 RAG); 6 Related Work; 7 Conclusion" — [2024.emnlp-demo.10](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-demo.10.pdf); Ai2 Scholar QA — "1 Introduction; 2 Pipeline (Retrieval, Reranking, Multi-step Generation); 3 Interface and Source Code; 4 Evaluation (Retrieval, Reranking, Generation, Real-world Usage and User Feedback); 5 Related Work; 6 Conclusion" — [2025.acl-demo.49](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-demo.49.pdf); ELLA — "2 Framework and Usage Example (Chat Interface, Interactive Legal Article Selection, Response Interpretation, Case Retrieval); 3 System Overview; 4 Evaluation (4.1 Automatic Evaluation, 4.2 User Study); 5 Conclusion" — [2024.acl-demos.36](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-demos.36.pdf); PledgeTracker — "3 PledgeTracker (Evidence retrieval, Timeline Construction); 4 User Interface Design; 5 Experiments (5.2 Evaluation in Real-world Use, 5.3 Comparison with Existing Tools, 5.4 Qualitative Feedback)" — [2025.emnlp-demos.64](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-demos.64.pdf); Marcel — "2 System Description (User Interaction; Software Architecture and Deployment); 3 Technical Evaluation (Experimental Systems, Protocol, Results); 4 Insights from End-User Deployment; 5 Related Work" — [2025.emnlp-demos.13](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-demos.13.pdf)
- Several accepted demos include explicit "Limitations" and/or "Ethical Considerations/Discussion" sections (RAGViz, VeraCT Scan, DOCMASTER, LocalRQA, InspectorRAGet, CiteLab) — PDFs cited in KQ3
- The ACL CFP's eight questions (problem, importance/impact, novelty, target audience, how it works, comparison with existing systems, license, evaluation/user studies) function as a de facto section checklist — [ACL 2026 demo CFP](https://2026.aclweb.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Chairs encourage "visual aids such as screenshots or diagrams" — [ACL 2025Q3 Demo Chairs report](https://www.aclweb.org/adminwiki/index.php/2025Q3_Reports:_Demo_Chairs)

### Inferences
- Space budget observed in the papers above: roughly 1 page intro, 1.5–2 pages UI/usage walkthrough with screenshots, 1.5 pages architecture, 1–1.5 pages evaluation, 0.5 page related work, 0.25 page conclusion. Evaluation is therefore ~20–25% of the content, not the 40–50% typical of a main-track paper.
- A Figure 1 that is a full-width annotated screenshot of the UI (or a UI + pipeline composite) appears in essentially every accepted RAG/QA demo; an architecture diagram is the usual Figure 2.
- Under the new 2-page appendix cap, the long appendices seen in SpiritRAG/LocalRQA (8–10 pages) are no longer possible; prompts, extra screenshots and full benchmark tables must be trimmed or moved to the repo.

### Gaps
- No official guidance on how many figures or how much evaluation a demo paper "should" have exists; the numbers above are empirical from 21 papers.

## Key Question 5: Common reasons demo papers are rejected

### Takeaway
Official sources only enumerate desk-reject causes (no link, no evaluation, over-length/format, >25% overlap, missing materials, ARR double submission, commercial pitch) and the chairs' complaint about text-to-speech or silent videos; substantive rejection reasons (weak novelty, "just an API wrapper," no NLP relevance, incomplete system) must be inferred from the CFP criteria, since demo reviews are not public.

### Cited Findings
- Desk reject: no live demo link/installable package (unless justified as impossible) — [NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Desk reject: "fail to provide any form of evaluation" — [NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Desk reject: exceeding 6 pages / 2-page appendix or not following style — [ACL 2026 demo CFP](https://2026.aclweb.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Desk reject: >25% overlap; identical work to ARR and demo track rejected by all tracks — [NAACL 2025 demo CFP](https://2025.naacl.org/calls/demo/); [NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Not accepted: systems that mainly wrap existing APIs / assemble off-the-shelf models without substantive contribution (NAACL 2027 wording) — [NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Not accepted: commercial sales and marketing content — [ACL 2025 demo CFP](https://2025.aclweb.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Not accepted: previously reported systems lacking >30% improvement (NAACL 2024/2025 rule; not restated in 2027 but the 25% overlap rule covers much of it) — [NAACL 2024 demo CFP](https://2024.naacl.org/calls/demos/)
- Not accepted: papers on sensitive data/tasks that omit ethics discussion — [ACL 2025 demo CFP](https://2025.aclweb.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Missing required materials invalidated 6/114 (2024), 9/187 (2025), 12/227 (2026) ACL demo submissions — [2024.acl-demos.0](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-demos.0.pdf); [2025.acl-demo.0](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-demo.0.pdf); [2026.acl-demo.0](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-demo.0.pdf)
- Chairs flagged "several submitted videos were done using text-to-speech or no voice over" and recommended prohibiting them — [ACL 2025Q3 Demo Chairs report](https://www.aclweb.org/adminwiki/index.php/2025Q3_Reports:_Demo_Chairs)
- Review criteria that substantive rejections are measured against: relevance to the demo track, contribution, clarity, completeness, novelty (NAACL 2024/2025); validity, quality, relevance to NLP (ACL 2025+/NAACL 2027) — [NAACL 2025 demo CFP](https://2025.naacl.org/calls/demo/); [NAACL 2027 demo CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/system_demonstration/)
- Decisions are also constrained by "the number of demo slots" set by the PCs — [ACL Demonstration Chair Handbook 2025](https://acl-org.github.io/conference-handbook/2025/demo)

### Inferences
- For the conflict-aware RAG prototype, the two highest-risk rejection vectors are (1) being read as an LLM+vector-store wrapper if the conflict-detection/evidence-inspection logic is not foregrounded with a comparison to a plain-RAG baseline, and (2) an evaluation judged thin if it rests solely on 30 synthetic questions. Adding a baseline comparison on the same 30 questions plus a small user/expert study (even 5–10 participants, as ELLA/My Climate CoPilot/SpiritRAG did) addresses both.
- "Completeness of the live demo" is a stated award criterion and likely an implicit review criterion: reviewers expect the hosted system to work end-to-end on their own inputs during Dec–Feb.

### Gaps
- No published analysis of substantive demo-track rejection reasons (e.g., aggregated reviewer comments) was found for any *ACL venue; demo-track reviews are not public on OpenReview.
- No chair blog posts or social-media threads about demo-track decisions were found.
